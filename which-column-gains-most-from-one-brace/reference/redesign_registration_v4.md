## New question and claim boundary

The same 3 m, equal-area, pinned, one-plane column system receives one identical midspan brace. Is the **solid round rod** the clearly largest **matched one-wave relative first-yield beneficiary** among the four original published shapes? Here R always divides braced one-wave first yield by the **same shape's bare one-wave first yield**. Separately, does the rod remain the leader in the conservative adverse-wave benchmark Rw, which divides the lesser of braced one-/two-wave first yield by that same **bare one-wave** reference? Rw is **not** the causal benefit for a matched two-wave intervention. Do not claim an ordinal ranking of the other three, a unique least beneficiary, or that two-wave bare-to-braced benefit was measured for every section. Report all four numerical values; call round tube and box a near-tied bottom pair under the adverse benchmark. The visual story is the rod's stability-driven headroom versus the box/tube's proximity to the 1.00 MN cross-section squash ceiling, not an optimum brace design. The rod still has the lowest nominal *absolute* braced first-yield load.

The production nominal values and 27 sensitivity conditions already exist and were observed. They are **discovery/description**, not a fresh prospective confirmation. They can supply the displayed 80-element loads and solver fields if the new checks pass. Two new uncomputed parameter points, fixed here and reviewed before running, are prospective checks *inside the explored parameter domain*, not independent confirmation or experimental evidence. Publication must disclose the redesign and failed v3 tie gate in the report. The video need not discuss the workflow, but must not say the box uniquely gains least or that the whole ordering is robust. Any adverse benchmark on screen must explicitly say `vs bare one-wave` and display **all four** values, including the near-tied bottom pair; rod and box alone would imply a hidden last-place rank.
